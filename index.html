Clean room for agent traces

Teams building agents on sensitive data can't monitor or improve them.

Legal, finance, and healthcare teams can't read their own production traces — the traces contain customer data. So the traffic that would show what their agent gets wrong never gets looked at.

Orizon is a clean room for those traces. Your production logs go in; a synthetic, structurally-identical dataset comes out — safe to read, cluster, replay, and eval, without exposing real data.

The problem

    

      

        Production traces are how teams build evals: you sample real traffic, find where the
        agent fails, turn those cases into a golden set, and catch regressions before your
        customers do.
      

      

        But if your data is sensitive, no one is allowed to look. Teams end up
        hiring people to hand-write fake traces that stand in for real
        users — slow, expensive, and nothing like what production actually throws at the agent.
        Most just fly blind.
      

    

  


  
  

    
What Orizon is

    

      

        
Orizon is a clean room for production agent traffic.

        
You evaluate your agent on real production behavior. No person — inside your company
          or ours — ever reads a real user's data.

      

      

        
Orizon is a golden eval set that builds itself from real traffic.

        
Instead of paying a team to hand-write test cases, you get a synthetic set drawn from
          what your users actually do, and the failures they actually hit.

      

      

        
Orizon is production observability for teams who can't read production.

        
Cluster real behavior, find the failure modes, and watch for regressions on the
          traffic you were never able to look at.

      

      

        
Orizon is a drop-in for the eval stack you already run.

        
Replay your agent on the synthetic traces, or export the set straight into Braintrust,
          Langfuse, or LangSmith.

      

    

  


  
  

    
How it fits

    

      
Orizon runs as a proxy or a passive SDK in front of your agent — nothing about how you
        build changes. Traces are handled in memory and never stored in the clear. What you keep
        is the synthetic dataset.
